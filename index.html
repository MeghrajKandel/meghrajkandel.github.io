About Me

Meghraj Kandel
AI Researcher

I am an AI researcher specialising in tourism demand forecasting using large language models. My work sits at the intersection of applied AI, time series analysis, and context engineering — building systems that do not just predict, but reason.

My primary research is the ATLAS framework — Adaptive Tourism LLM with Aligned Signals — a three-module system that addresses context misalignment in LLM-based forecasting. ATLAS combines regime-adaptive context selection, STL-decomposed demand signals, and Weighted Permutation Entropy-based uncertainty calibration to improve forecast accuracy on real-world tourism datasets.

I am currently completing a Master of ICT (Research) at Melbourne Institute of Technology, where this work forms the core of my thesis. I am passionate about research that is rigorous, honest, and practically useful.

Research

Master's Thesis · Active Research
ATLAS Framework

ATLAS addresses a fundamental problem in LLM-based forecasting — context misalignment. Standard approaches give the LLM a generic historical window, raw search data, and no uncertainty guidance. ATLAS corrects this through three dedicated modules: regime-adaptive context selection (Module 1), STL-decomposed Google Trends narrative features (Module 2), and Weighted Permutation Entropy uncertainty calibration (Module 3). The result is a context-aligned LLM forecasting system that reasons with the right information at every step.

Key Results
Hong Kong
ATLAS achieves a 10.5% improvement over Holt-Winters at h=1 and a 37.1% improvement at h=3 — significantly outperforming unstructured GPT Static (p=0.0033). Regime-adaptive context selection accounts for 42.7% of the framework's MAE advantage.
Macau
On a stable, seasonally regular market, Holt-Winters remains the dominant standalone model. ATLAS significantly outperforms all LLM and machine learning baselines (p<0.01), demonstrating that context engineering adds value even where the statistical benchmark is hard to beat.
